# Case Study 1 — IQAC Activity Portal (MCET NAAC Activity Portal)

**Source:** https://dexoshouse.com/Blog-style%20website%20flow/IQAC%20Portal%20Story.dc.html
**Note:** This page is a client-rendered template; many sections (step descriptions, role blurbs, module text) were unrendered placeholders when fetched, so only the visible static content is captured below.

## Summary
- **Name:** IQAC Activity Portal / MCET NAAC Activity Portal
- **Client:** Dr. Mahalingam College of Engineering and Technology (MCET), Internal Quality Assurance Cell (IQAC)
- **Category:** Case study · Institutional software
- **Scale:** 12 departments · 3 activity categories · 4 roles
- **Headline:** "One portal in place of a hundred and fifty spreadsheets."
- **Description:** Turns a manual, Excel-driven quality-assurance process into a role-based digital workflow — task assignment, faculty submission, three levels of verification, and analytics that update themselves.
- **One-line pitch:** A web-based IQAC activity management portal that digitizes the complete process of task assignment, faculty submission, multi-level verification, approval, tracking, analytics, and reporting, replacing the existing manual Excel-based workflow.

## The problem
Nearly 150 integrated sheets were maintained by hand. Faculty typed activity records into spreadsheets. IQAC could not verify every entry, HODs could not see who had finished, and answering "how many activities are approved?" meant opening file after file.

## Roles (4)
HOD and SPOC are also faculty members — they switch views rather than hold separate accounts. IQAC Admin is the super admin. Roles: Faculty, SPOC, HOD, IQAC Admin.

## Workflow
- Eight stages: assignment travels downward, verification travels back up.
- Three verification levels: SPOC → HOD → IQAC.
- Any reviewer (SPOC, HOD, or IQAC) can reject a submission with a written remark; the record returns to the faculty member, who corrects and resubmits into the same chain.
- Every submission carries a visible status (status ladder).
- Record path across screens: Submit New Activity → All Department Activities (SPOC) → Pending Approvals (HOD) → All Activities (IQAC).

## Screens / features shown
- IQAC Analytics Dashboard
- HOD view — department targets, Pending Approvals, Assign Faculty Target
- Faculty Dashboard
- Activity Count by Department; Department Target Progress ("Achieving goals set by IQAC"); Completed Targets
- Example record shown: FDP-STTP / Faculty Development Review, Dr. L. Meenachi (HOD), 13 Jul 2026, #72
- Diagrams: role hierarchy, eight-stage swimlane, approval loop ("One record, one database, one status"), data flow ("Nothing is entered twice")

# Case Study 2 — MRPC (Monthly Research Progress Connect)

**Source:** https://dexoshouse.com/Blog-style%20website%20flow/MRPC%20Case%20Study%20standalone-src.html

## Summary
- **Project:** Monthly Research Progress Connect (MRPC)
- **Client / Institution:** Dr. Mahalingam College of Engineering and Technology (MCET)
- **Category:** Research management, progress tracking
- **Platform:** Web application
- **Headline:** "Making research progress visible, structured and connected."
- **Description:** Brings research progress, activities, project information and monitoring into one centralized digital workspace — from the target a department is set, to the record a faculty member submits, to the report the institution prints.

## The challenge
Research offices run on a continuous cycle (targets set → faculty do the work → someone verifies → institution answers for it). When that lives in documents and mail threads, each stage loses the one before it.
1. **Scattered information** — publications, patents, funding and consultancy records sit with individual faculty in separate files.
2. **Manual follow-up** — verifying work means chasing people, not opening a screen.
3. **Limited visibility** — a department cannot see how far it is from a target in time to act.
4. **Difficult monitoring** — consolidating across twelve departments and five faculty cadres is rebuilt by hand every reporting cycle.

## The solution — eight capabilities
1. **Target definition** — targets set per department, research sheet and academic year, with a target count.
2. **Target assignment** — assigned to a department and on to faculty, across fourteen research work fields.
3. **Activity submission** — three-step record: choose category, choose activity sheet, fill details.
4. **Review & status** — pending, approved, rejected counts per department; status on every record.
5. **Points matrix** — a published RPP (Research Performance Points) matrix maps each activity category to points; points for multi-author activities are divided equally among MCET contributors.
6. **Department consolidation** — criterion-wise summary across Q1–Q4 with totals, exportable per department.
7. **Faculty progress** — achievement score, target progress and full submission history per faculty member.
8. **Reports** — RI 600 progress report, faculty self-appraisal analysis, and a full institutional MRPC report.

## Users & roles (4 views of the same record)
Dean (R&I), HOD, SPOC, Faculty. They are views, not walled-off products — the Dean's header has a "My Faculty View" switch ("Switched to FACULTY view") with an "Open Dean View" control to return.

| Role | Level | Sees | Manages | Monitors |
|------|-------|------|---------|----------|
| Dean (R&I) | Institution | Institution-wide counts, output by department, portfolio by activity type, all 12 departmental streams | Department research targets; institutional assignment form | Every institutional research submission and status; generates full MRPC report |
| HOD | Department | Department targets with achieved-vs-needed progress; all department activities | Set Target, Assign Faculty; faculty records under Manage Faculties | Pending approvals; consolidation summary by criterion and quarter; department export |
| SPOC | Department · Review | Department approval dashboard: total, pending, approved, rejected | User management for the department; points matrix reference | Pending approvals; full department activity list with status and last update |
| Faculty | Individual | My Activity Dashboard — achievement points, submission counts, assigned targets | New submissions via 3-step form | Status and remarks per submission; completed targets; RI 600 report |

Hierarchy: Dean (R&I) → HOD / Department → SPOC / Review → Faculty / Research activity → Research progress.

## Workflow (7 stages)
1. Target is defined (Dean R&I) — against a department, research sheet and academic year, with a target count.
2. Target is assigned
3. Faculty submits an activity
4. Department review
5. Status is carried
6. Points are applied
7. Reporting

## Dashboard (Dean R&I Overview)
- Outcome counters: total studies, publications, IPR & patents, external funding.
- "Generate Full MRPC Report" button, "Set Research Target", and faculty-view switch.
- Inter-departmental research output chart.
- Research portfolio breakdown by activity type — journal, IPR, conference, funding, consultancy.
- Twelve department cards that act as filters for the tables below.

## Activity records — eighteen activity sheets
Journal Publications, Book Publication, Conference, Patent, Research-Submitted, Research-Preparation, IPR-Preparation, Research Project, Research-Funding-Submitted, Consultancy (Project), Consultancy (Training), Consultancy-Planning, Students In-house Seed Money, Technology Transfer, Expert Certification, Skill Services, Conference Organization, and PhD Completion.
- Categories shown in All Department Activities: Research or Faculty Development.
- Same row readable at institution, department and individual levels.

## RI 600 report (Monthly Research Progress Connect report)
- Header: faculty name, staff ID, department, designation, over a stated period and calendar year.
- Six counters: Published (1.1), Submitted (1.2), Preparation (1.3), IPR Details (2), Funding (3.1), Consultancy (3.2); each has a detail tab, ending at PRO 600.
- Tab 1.1 lists indexed article details: type, authors, title, journal/conference name, publisher, volume and page, month and year, DOI and indexing.
- Only verified records reach the report.

## Targets & assignment
- "Assign Department Research Targets" form: target department, academic year, target count, one research work field from fourteen options, then "Update Department Assignment".
- HOD sees department targets with percentage, achieved-of-total ratio and what's still needed.
- Faculty see the same target as "My Targets" (auto-updating; pending and completed).
- Nothing is re-entered between levels.

## Faculty experience
- My Activity Dashboard: achievement points, counters for all/pending/approved/rejected, submissions with status and remarks.
- Submit New Activity: three numbered steps.
- Self-Appraisal Analysis: printable performance record per academic year, criterion-wise tables, scores under Research and Development.
- Sidebar: Submit activity, Reports (completed targets), Self report (appraisal), Research progress (RI 600).

## Monitoring (HOD / SPOC)
- SPOC view: four counters (total in department, pending action, approved, rejected).
- Consolidation Summary criteria include Q1-indexed journals, Scopus/SCI/WOS, conference publications, book chapters, seed money, patents filed and granted, copyrights, grants — across Q1–Q4 with total, exportable.
- Manage Faculties: register faculty with name, ID and academic cadre; users listed by department, grouped by cadre with member count.
- Status labels include: Approved by R&D, Dean (R&I) Approved, OD Approved (Awaiting Proof).

## Generated report (Generate Full MRPC Report)
Nine-section institutional report from the Office of Dean Research & Innovation (sample for a 2026 period): cover with institutional score vs achievement; summary of research outcome (publications by indexing, IPR by type, technology transfer, funding and consultancy amounts); departmentwise ranking with heatmap; faculty cadre analysis (count, target, achieved, gap); top performers; index; through to cadre performance and department toppers.

## Design system
Warm palette: Navigation #2E1B0E (deep brown), Primary action #E8420A (orange), Page #FAF6F2, Surface #FFFFFF. Status badges (Approved, Dean (R&I) Approved, Pending, Rejected) are the only extra colour. One solid orange button per screen region.

## Impact (7 outcomes)
1. Centralized research information
2. Structured progress tracking
3. Better activity visibility (eighteen defined sheets)
4. Easier monitoring (counters on a dashboard)
5. Clearer departmental view (criterion-wise, per quarter, exportable)
6. Transparent points (published matrix and sharing rule)
7. Reporting readiness (institutional, departmental, individual reports from verified records)

# Case Study 3 — KonguBusiness Forum (KBF Pollachi Official Website)

## Project at a glance

| Client | Kongu Business Forum (KBF), Pollachi |
| Project | KBF Pollachi – Official Business Forum Website |

Badges: 14 Days · 4 Phases · 6 Team Members · HTML / CSS / JavaScript · Live at kbfpollachi.com.

## Background
KBF needed an official website for its business community — one public home for members, events and enquiries. The team delivered a responsive static website in 2 weeks, from requirement analysis to deployment and client approval.

## Pages shipped
Five pages, one event detail view, shared header and footer. Navy and gold theme, Playfair Display for headings, photography from KBF's own events.
1. **Home** — hero with forum positioning, membership call to action, live counts for members, networks, events and industries.
2. **About Us** — vision statement and KBF management committee grid.
3. **Events** — hero with three event types; filterable event cards by category.
4. **Event detail** — opens in place (modal); shows category, date, location, organiser.
5. **Gallery** — events, meetings and industrial visits.
6. **Footer** — quick links, contact details, social channels.

## 4-phase workflow (14 days)
| Phase | Duration | Name |
|-------|----------|------|
| 01 | 2 days | Requirement Analysis & Planning |
| 02 | 5 days | UI/UX Design & AI-Assisted Development |
| 03 | 4 days | Testing, Bug Fixing & Deployment |
| 04 | 3 days | Final Review & Content Updates |

### Phase 01 — Requirement analysis & planning (2 days)
- Did: discussed requirements, objectives, target audience; identified key pages, features and content; reviewed existing content, branding and reference sites; finalized scope, technology approach and timeline.
- Constraints: 2-week timeline; client confirmation needed for some content/images; AI-generated code needed manual review and testing; responsive compatibility required.
- Success criteria: completed within 2 weeks; required pages and functionality; responsive and user-friendly; client approval; successful deployment.
- Owner: KBF Pollachi client + development team.
- Takeaway: two days of scoping saved more than two days later.

### Phase 02A — UI/UX design
- Done in Figma. Flow: Requirements → Wireframes → Figma UI/UX → Visual Design → Responsive Design → Client Feedback → Final Design.
- Checked at desktop, tablet, mobile. Revisions covered layout, spacing, typography, colors, navigation, client feedback, content changes.
- Owner: UI/UX Designer & Development Team.

### User flows
1. Visitor → Home → About / Events / Gallery → Contact
2. Visitor → Join Forum → Registration Form → Submit
3. Visitor → Events → Event Details → Registration / Contact
4. Admin / Team → Update Content → Review → Publish

### Phase 02B — AI-assisted development
Principle: "AI-assisted, human-reviewed development." Process: AI-generated basic prototype → Review → Customize → Test → Iterate → Finalize.
- **Claude AI** — planning, content structuring, code assistance.
- **ChatGPT** — requirement analysis, prompt engineering, troubleshooting.
- All AI-generated code was manually reviewed, tested and customized; the website is not fully AI-generated.

### Technical approach
- Frontend: HTML, CSS, JavaScript
- Backend: none (static website)
- Database: none specified
- APIs: none custom
- Version control: not used — developed and updated locally
- Tooling: Figma (design), Claude AI & ChatGPT (AI), MilesWeb (hosting)

### Phase 03 — Testing, bug fixing & deployment (4 days)
- Tested on desktop, tablet, mobile and major browsers. Performance: basic checks only. Security: basic review, no formal security testing.
- Bugs found: minor responsive layout issues; incorrect/outdated content and image placement; minor navigation and UI alignment issues.
- Deployment: hosting configuration → domain configuration → deployment → live. Deployment completed within the 2-week timeline. Monitoring and analytics were not implemented.

### Phase 04 — Final review & content updates (3 days)
Client feedback → content updates → UI corrections → bug corrections → final review → approval. Result: a final website approved by the client.

## Handover & team
Handed over after deployment with website source files and deployment details. Post-launch, minor updates and content changes are made as required. Team roles credited: Founder/CEO, COO, CPO, UI/UX Designer, Lead Developer, CFO. Contributions: planning, design, content, development, testing, deployment.

## Outcome
- Primary: completed, client-approved and deployed within 2 weeks.
- Secondary: responsive, user-friendly, functional on desktop, tablet and mobile.
- Checklist: 14-day completion ✓ · required pages and functionality ✓ · client approval ✓ · successfully deployed ✓.

## Lessons
1. A clear 4-phase plan made 14-day delivery achievable.
2. AI speeds up development, but human review and testing are essential.
3. Early client input on content reduces late-stage corrections.
